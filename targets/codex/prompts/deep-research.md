# Deep research

Establish the question, intended decision, scope, and budget from the request. Ask only for information that materially changes the research. Split the question into testable subquestions, including evidence that could refute the preferred answer.

Use available web search and primary-source fetch tools. Exa and Firecrawl are optional adapters, not requirements. Discover their actual schemas when configured. Do not auto-install a crawler or send private project content in queries.

For each subquestion:

1. Search using public technical concepts and competing formulations. Prefer original papers, official docs, source code, and specifications.
2. Open the source; record title, URL, publication date, retrieval date, methods, supporting passage or result, and limits. Search snippets and abstracts do not establish detailed claims.
3. Follow citations to the evidence owner. Separate independent corroboration from several sites repeating the same source.
4. Maintain a claim ledger with supported, disputed, inferred, and unverified states. Investigate disagreements in population, version, measurement, and dates before averaging conclusions.
5. Stop when the decision-relevant questions have evidence or a documented access gap. Do not pad the report with low-quality results to meet a source count.

Deliver the answer first, then evidence, competing explanations, remaining uncertainty, and any proposed experiment. Cite near the claim. Save an artifact only when requested or required by the repository workflow. For long-lived execution use `run-durable-research`; for applying digest evidence use `digest-workflow-research`.
